SJF (Preemptive) Analysis

Always picks shortest remaining burst. Typically minimizes average wait time overall but causes many context switches.

SJF (Non-Preemptive) Analysis

Once a job starts, it finishes. Less context switching, but a short job arriving later must wait for a long running job.

Priority Analysis

Favors low priority numbers. Can cause starvation for low priority tasks.

Final Conclusion

Conclusion & Recommendation

Based on the simulation results: SJF(Pre) achieved better average waiting time, SJF(Pre) achieved better average turnaround time, and SJF(Pre) had better average response time.

Efficiency vs. Urgency Trade-off: SJF algorithms focus on overall efficiency by selecting the shortest jobs to minimize average waiting times. In contrast, Priority scheduling focuses on urgency by ensuring critical tasks run first, even if it sacrifices overall system efficiency and increases the wait times for other processes. SJF(Preemptive) usually offers the lowest waiting time but adds overhead due to preemption, while SJF(Non-Preemptive) provides a balance with fewer context switches.
